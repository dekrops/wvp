# RFC 0003: WVP v0.3

|            |                                                                                                                            |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RFC        | 0003                                                                                                                       |
| Status     | Draft                                                                                                                      |
| Author     | Zin Nam                                                                                                                    |
| Created    | 2026-10-12                                                                                                                 |
| Affects    | A new specification, `wvp-0.3`. `wvp-0.1`, `wvp-0.2`, `work-contract-1` and `verified-evidence-statement-v1` are unchanged |
| Supersedes | Nothing                                                                                                                    |

A new version of the package protocol that closes four gaps of `wvp-0.2`, and nothing else: the claimant signs the claim; a completeness statement says which claim and which records it covers; a result says whether the contract was the relying party's; and an issuer's signature is an endorsement a relying party may require, not a condition of reproducing a decision. This RFC proposes the design for review. It does not publish `wvp-0.3`.

## Motivation

[The open questions](../governance/open-questions.md) A1, A3 and A4, and [the audit](../governance/normative-audit-2026-10.md), section 4. Each is a way to rely on a `wvp-0.2` result for more than it establishes.

| #   | In `wvp-0.2`                                                                                                                                                                                              | Who can use it                                                                                              |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G1  | A package carries its own contract, and expectations are optional. A verification that pins nothing reports `SUPPORTED` for a contract its author wrote to be satisfied                                   | Anyone who can obtain one signed record from a registered source, about anything                            |
| G2  | The claim is not signed. Nobody is bound to having made it, and whoever assembles a package can write any claim over evidence it holds                                                                    | An assembler; a claimant who later denies the claim                                                         |
| G3  | A completeness statement is `{ kind, asOf }`. It names no claim and no set of records. A record can be left out of a package after the source declared its listing complete, and the kind is still closed | Whoever assembles the package. The decision can become `SUPPORTED` where the source's records contradict it |
| G4  | One accepted source's completeness statement closes a kind that the contract accepts from two                                                                                                             | An assembler holding one source's statement and omitting the other source's records                         |
| G5  | A claim lists evidence by identifier. A statement can be exchanged for another with the same identifier                                                                                                   | A source and an assembler together                                                                          |
| G6  | An issuer signature under a key the relying party does not hold refuses the whole package, and an issuer signature is the only thing that binds anyone to `inputs.json`                                   | Nobody gains; relying parties with different registries cannot verify one countersigned package             |

## Threat model

**The parties.** A _claimant_ asserts that work was done. _Sources_ sign what they observed. An _assembler_ puts a package together; it may be the claimant, an issuer, or anyone holding the parts. An _issuer_ may sign the decision. A _relying party_ verifies, under a registry and expectations that are its own.

**The adversary** controls every byte of the package, and may be the claimant, the assembler, or both. It may hold keys the relying party registered for some sources and not others. It may replay any object it has seen, in any tenant.

**Assumed.** Ed25519 and SHA-256 hold. The relying party's registry and expectations are what it intends, and reach the verifier unaltered. A key's holder is the party the registry names until the registry says otherwise.

**Not assumed, and not established by this version.** That a source tells the truth; that a source's listing is complete because it says so; that any time in a package is the time something happened. Every time is asserted by whoever signed it.

## What changes

The container, the canonical encoding, evidence statements, the trust registry and Work Contract Language 1 are those of `wvp-0.2`, with the rules of [RFC 0002](0002-inputs-every-verifier-refuses-alike.md) applied from the start. Seven things differ.

### 1. The claim is signed

`claim.json` is `{ "claim": { … }, "signature": { "scheme", "keyId", "encoded" } }`. The claim's members are those of `wvp-0.2`, with `evidence` changed (below).

- **Signing input:** `verified:wvp-claim:v0.3\n` followed by the canonical encoding of the `claim` member. The prefix differs from every other signing domain, and names the version, so the signature is valid for nothing else.
- **What it binds.** The claim holds `id`, `tenant`, the `contract` digest, the `inputs`, `claimant`, `evidence` and `submittedAt`. A signature over it binds the claimant to this contract, these inputs, this tenant and exactly this evidence.
- **Who may sign.** A key counts for a claim if the relying party's registry holds it, for the claim's `tenant`, with `issuer` equal to the claim's `claimant`, with the source `wvp-claim` among its sources, valid at `submittedAt`. No registry member is added.
- **`wvp-claim` and `wvp-decision` are roles, not systems.** A statement whose `source.system` is either name is refused (`RESERVED_SOURCE`). So a key authorized only to make claims cannot sign evidence, and a key authorized only for a source cannot sign a claim. A relying party can still give one key both; the report says when it has (change 6).

The signature is required. A package without one is not a `wvp-0.3` package; a claim nobody signed remains expressible in `wvp-0.2`.

### 2. A claim names its evidence by digest

`claim.evidence` is an array of `{ "id", "statement" }`, ascending by `id`, where `statement` is the digest of the signed statement's canonical `statement` member (its statement digest, as Evidence Statement v1 defines). Admission requires each statement in the package to be one the claim lists, by identifier **and** digest.

### 3. A completeness statement says what it covers

A `complete` body is:

```json
{
  "complete": {
    "kind": "reversal",
    "asOf": "1767225600",
    "inputs": { "algorithm": "sha-256", "hex": "…" },
    "records": { "count": 2, "digest": { "algorithm": "sha-256", "hex": "…" } }
  }
}
```

- `inputs` is the digest of the canonical encoding of the claim's `inputs` object: the question the listing answers. A source vouches for "my records of this kind, for these inputs", and not for a kind in general.
- `records.digest` is the SHA-256 of the canonical encoding of the JSON array of the statement digests of every `record` statement of that kind that this source issued for these inputs, as lowercase hexadecimal strings in ascending order. `records.count` is the length of that array.

The verifier computes the same array from the package: the admitted `record` statements of that kind whose `source.system` is the completeness statement's. If the count or the digest differs, the package is refused. A record cannot be left out, or added, after the source declared its listing.

### 4. A kind is closed by every source the contract accepts for it

The environment passed to Work Contract Language 1 holds a completeness statement for a kind only if, for **each** source the contract accepts for that kind, the package holds an admitted completeness statement from that source. Otherwise it holds none for that kind, and the kind is open. Where a source has several, the latest `asOf` stands, as in `wvp-0.2`.

The language is unchanged: section 7, step 6 of its specification closes a kind on any accepted source's statement, and this version decides which statements reach it.

### 5. Issuer signatures are endorsements

- A signature in `signatures` under a key the registry does not hold is **not counted** and is not a refusal. The report lists it as unrecognized.
- A signature under a key the registry holds must count, as in `wvp-0.2` section 6, with the signing domain `verified:wvp-decision:v0.3\n`. If it does not, the package is refused (`ISSUER_SIGNATURE_INVALID`).
- No issuer signature is needed to reproduce a decision. A relying party that wants one requires it by an expectation (change 7).

No signature is added over the manifest or `inputs.json`. The decision commits to the claim, the contract, the evidence and the inputs by digest, so a signature over the decision already covers them.

### 6. What a report states

A report states each of these separately, and no single "valid":

| Property                | Established when                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `integrity`             | Steps 1 to 7                                                                                                                 |
| `claimAuthenticated`    | Step 9: the claim is signed by a key the registry authorizes for this claimant and tenant                                    |
| `evidenceAuthenticated` | Step 10: every statement is signed by a key the registry authorizes for its source                                           |
| `completeness`          | A list: for each closed kind, each source, its `asOf` and its count. Never "complete" without the source and the time        |
| `decisionReproduced`    | Steps 11 to 14                                                                                                               |
| `issuerEndorsements`    | The issuers whose signatures counted, and the key IDs that were not recognized                                               |
| `claimantAlsoSource`    | The sources of admitted statements signed by a key whose `issuer` is the claimant. Empty when the claimant evidenced nothing |
| `expectations`          | For each of `tenant`, `contract`, `claim`, `claimant`, `issuer`: `MET` or `NOT_GIVEN`                                        |
| `standing`              | `REPRODUCED` or `ESTABLISHED`                                                                                                |

**`standing`** is the answer to G1. It is `ESTABLISHED` when every check passed **and** the relying party gave, and the package met, the expectations `tenant` and `contract`. Otherwise it is `REPRODUCED`: the decision is what the package's own contract yields from its evidence, and nothing says the contract is one the relying party agreed to.

Two rules follow, and bind every implementation:

1. An interface that gates on a decision (an exit code, a `require` call) MUST fail on a result whose standing is not `ESTABLISHED`.
2. A verifier MUST NOT describe a `REPRODUCED` result with a word that suggests acceptance. `standing` and every `NOT_GIVEN` are part of the report, including in its shortest form.

### 7. Expectations

As `wvp-0.2` section 3.1, checked after every other check, in this order: `tenant`, `contract`, `claim`, then two new ones: `claimant` (the claim's `claimant` is the expected one, otherwise `CLAIMANT_NOT_EXPECTED`) and `issuer` (some counted issuer signature is under a key whose `issuer` is the expected one, otherwise `ISSUER_NOT_EXPECTED`). An expectation that is not given is not checked, and nothing in a package can supply one.

## The verification algorithm

Checks run in this order and stop at the first failure. Steps 1 to 8 and 13 to 15 are those of `wvp-0.2`, renumbered.

| Step | Check                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Refusal                                                                                           |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–7  | Canonical text; version (`protocol` is `wvp-0.3`); members; component set; component digests; commitment; every component well-formed, with the shapes of changes 1 to 3                                      | As `wvp-0.2`                                                                                      |
|      | **Integrity is established. A verifier without a registry stops here.**                                                                                                                                       |                                                                                                   |
| 8    | The decision names this protocol and the `work-contract` evaluator                                                                                                                                            | `UNSUPPORTED_EVALUATOR`                                                                           |
| 9    | The claim's signature: its key is in the registry; the key is for the claim's tenant, its `issuer` is the claimant and its sources include `wvp-claim`; it was valid at `submittedAt`; the signature verifies | `CLAIM_KEY_UNKNOWN`, `CLAIM_KEY_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `CLAIM_KEY_NOT_VALID`, `CLAIM_SIGNATURE_INVALID` |
| 10   | Each statement verifies under the registry, in order; no statement's source is a reserved name                                                                                                                | The statement's code; `RESERVED_SOURCE`                                                           |
| 11   | Admission, as `wvp-0.2`, with the statements being exactly those the claim lists by identifier and digest                                                                                                     | The admission codes                                                                               |
| 12   | Each completeness statement: its `inputs` is the digest of the claim's inputs; its `records` is the count and digest of the admitted records of its kind from its source                                      | `COMPLETENESS_NOT_FOR_CLAIM`, `COMPLETENESS_SET_MISMATCH`                                         |
| 13   | Input conditions, as `wvp-0.2`                                                                                                                                                                                | `INPUT_MISMATCH`                                                                                  |
| 14   | The decision computed from the environment of changes 3 and 4 equals `decision.json`                                                                                                                          | `DECISION_MISMATCH`                                                                               |
| 15   | Every issuer signature under a registered key counts                                                                                                                                                          | `ISSUER_SIGNATURE_INVALID`                                                                        |
| 16   | Expectations, in the order of change 7                                                                                                                                                                        | The expectation's code                                                                            |

The claim is checked before the evidence because the evidence is admitted against the claim: until the claim is known to be the claimant's, its list of evidence is anyone's.

## Outcomes that change

Every one, in the sense of RFC 0001: `wvp-0.3` is a new identifier, in a new directory, with its own vectors (invariant 2). No `wvp-0.1` or `wvp-0.2` package, vector or expectation changes, and each version refuses the others' packages at step 2 (invariant 3).

New refusal codes: `CLAIM_KEY_UNKNOWN`, `CLAIM_KEY_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `CLAIM_KEY_NOT_VALID`, `CLAIM_SIGNATURE_INVALID`, `RESERVED_SOURCE`, `COMPLETENESS_NOT_FOR_CLAIM`, `COMPLETENESS_SET_MISMATCH`, `CLAIMANT_NOT_EXPECTED`, `ISSUER_NOT_EXPECTED`.

## Compatibility

- **Verifiers** of `wvp-0.1` and `wvp-0.2` are untouched and keep refusing a `wvp-0.3` package as an unsupported version. A verifier may implement any subset of the three.
- **Producers** need one new key role (a claimant key registered by the relying party under `wvp-claim`), and completeness statements issued for a claim's inputs. A source that cannot tell which of its records belong to a claim's inputs cannot issue a `wvp-0.3` completeness statement; kinds from that source stay open, and a contract that needs them closed yields `UNDETERMINED`. That is the honest result.
- **Relying parties** that verified without expectations get `REPRODUCED` where they used to read `SUPPORTED`, and a gate that used to pass fails. This is the intended break, and the reason the change is a version.
- **Migration.** `wvp-0.2` is not deprecated by this RFC. A package cannot be converted: the claimant must sign, and each source must issue its completeness statements again.
- **The other specifications.** Claim registration v1 and work relation v1 refer to packages by digest and to contracts by digest. Neither depends on the shape of a claim; work relation v1 lists the protocols of the records it relates and would need a revision to name `wvp-0.3`.

## What can be proven, and what cannot

About completeness, precisely, since it is the claim most easily overstated:

- **Established:** source _S_ signed, at the time it asserts, that for the inputs with digest _I_ its records of kind _K_ as of `asOf` are exactly the set with digest _D_; and the package holds exactly that set.
- **Not established:** that _S_ has no other record; that _S_ issued no later listing with more records (an assembler can present an older statement, and nothing in a package shows it is the latest); that sources the contract does not accept know nothing; that the listing is complete for any inputs but these.

So a `wvp-0.3` result says **source-scoped, input-scoped, time-bounded** completeness, by a named source. It never says the evidence is complete.

## Vectors

At least these, in `test-vectors/wvp/v0.3`, with `valid/`, `must-reject/` and `invalid/` as before:

- **Valid:** the three decisions; one with a counted issuer signature; one with an unrecognized issuer signature beside it (accepted, reported); integrity only; a kind accepted from two sources and closed by both; `claimantAlsoSource` not empty.
- **Standing:** expectation cases for every combination of `tenant` and `contract` given and not given, showing `REPRODUCED` and `ESTABLISHED`; `CLAIMANT_NOT_EXPECTED`; `ISSUER_NOT_EXPECTED` with no signature, with an unrecognized one, and with another issuer's.
- **Claim:** each of the four claim codes; a claim signed under the `wvp-0.2` decision domain and under the statement domain; a `wvp-0.2` claim (no signature) in a `wvp-0.3` container; a claim signed by a key registered only for a source; a claim for tenant A signed by tenant B's claimant key; a claim replayed with one statement exchanged for another of the same identifier.
- **Completeness:** a record left out after the listing (`COMPLETENESS_SET_MISMATCH`); a record added; a listing for other inputs (`COMPLETENESS_NOT_FOR_CLAIM`); a kind accepted from two sources with one listing, where the decision must be `UNDETERMINED` and a `SUPPORTED` decision is `DECISION_MISMATCH`; a count that is right with a digest that is wrong.
- **Reserved names:** a statement from `source.system` `wvp-claim`, correctly signed by a key registered for it.
- **Order of refusals:** a package failing steps 9 and 10 at once, and 12 and 14 at once, pinning the first.

How they are produced without the reference implementation is the subject of the authoring transition; until then they come from it, and a public implementation must reproduce them before this RFC is Implemented.

## Security considerations

**Prevented.**

- _A claim nobody made_ (G2): a claim verifies only under a key the relying party registered for that claimant and tenant.
- _Cross-tenant and cross-protocol replay:_ the claim holds its tenant and the key is registered for one; the signing domain names the object and the version.
- _Replay with other evidence_ (G5): the claim holds each statement's digest.
- _Omission after completeness_ (G3, G4): by an assembler who is not the source. The source's own signature fixes the set.
- _A supported decision under a contract the relying party never saw_ (G1) passing a gate.
- _Evidence signed in the role of a claim, or the reverse._

**Not prevented.**

- _A lying source,_ or a claimant and a source acting together.
- _An omitted completeness statement._ The kind is then open. By the stability theorem of the contract language (`definite_decision_is_stable`, with its hypotheses), removing a completeness statement cannot turn another decision into `SUPPORTED` or `CONTRADICTED`; it can only lose one.
- _An old listing._ See above.
- _Backdating._ Key validity is evaluated at `submittedAt`, which the signer asserts. The holder of a revoked claimant key can sign a claim dated before the revocation. A relying party that revokes a key because it was compromised should set `revokedAt` no later than `validFrom`, so that nothing verifies under it. Authenticated time is open question B1 and is deferred.
- _The same work claimed twice._ A new claim identifier is a new claim. That is what work-unit identity and claim registration are for.

**Tenant isolation.** A package verifies only if the claim's tenant, the evaluation's tenant, every statement's tenant and the tenant of every key used are one. `inputs` digests are not secret: a completeness statement reveals that a source was asked about some inputs, to whoever holds the statement.

**Primitives.** Ed25519 (RFC 8032, under the rule of RFC 0002), SHA-256 (FIPS 180-4). None is new.

**Review.** No person other than the author has reviewed this RFC. Under RFC 0001, section 9.1, it cannot become Final until a competent reviewer has, and is named here.

## Formal semantics

**Proved today, about what this version reuses:** the properties of Work Contract Language 1's evaluation listed in [`formal/lean`](../formal/lean/README.md), including that a decision is a function of contract and environment, and stability under extension. They are statements about the Lean definition.

**Worth proving for this version,** each small, about a model of steps 9 to 14 in which signatures and digests are assumed:

1. _Claim binding._ If a package is accepted, the claim was signed under a key authorized as claimant for the package's tenant, the claim's contract digest is the contract's, and the statements are exactly those the claim names.
2. _No omission under completeness._ If a package is accepted and a kind is closed in its environment, then for every source the contract accepts for that kind, the records of that kind from that source are exactly the set that source's statement commits to. Removing or adding one makes the package refused.
3. _Pins come from outside._ `standing` is `ESTABLISHED` only if the tenant and contract expectations were given. No content of the package changes which expectations were given.
4. _The added checks only refuse._ Every package this procedure accepts would be accepted by the same procedure without steps 9 and 12, with the same decision. So the new steps cannot make a decision that the evaluation would not.

**Proved with this RFC:** a model of these four is in [`0003/formal`](0003/formal), checked by this repository's CI. Its README says what is assumed.

**Tested only:** everything about encodings, digests and signatures.

## Alternatives

- **Make the claimant signature optional**, with an assurance level in the report. Two shapes of claim, two paths in every verifier, and a level to misread. An unsigned claim already has a version.
- **Require an issuer signature for an established result** (as the open questions suggest for A4). The relying party reproduces the decision itself, so an issuer adds accountability and not correctness. Requiring it would make every producer an issuer or depend on one, and give a relying party nothing it cannot get by pinning `issuer`.
- **Sign the manifest.** The decision already commits to every component a decision depends on.
- **Bind completeness to a stream position** in a chained, anchored stream. Stronger: it would show the listing is the latest. It needs anchoring (B1), which needs a registry. It is deferred, and nothing here prevents adding it as another form of `records`.
- **List the record digests** in the completeness statement, not their digest. Better diagnostics, unbounded size. The verifier has the records and can say which kind and source failed.
- **Change the contract language** so that a kind is closed per source. A new language version for something the layer above can decide.
- **Fail a verification without pins** instead of reporting `REPRODUCED`. Reproducing a decision without pins is useful to an auditor reading a package, and is honest when it is called what it is.

## Deferred, explicitly

Authenticated time and anchored stream heads (B1). A Merkle log (B2). Assurance per requirement (B3). Arithmetic in contracts (B4). A container for histories (B5). Evaluating records that are committed to and not disclosed, by any proof system. Units, duplicates and settlement. Resource limits (C5). None is needed for the four gaps, and none is made harder by this version.

## Open questions

1. **Is `tenant` and `contract` the right minimum for `ESTABLISHED`?** `claim` could be required too. It is not, because a relying party often agrees to a contract before it knows a claim's identifier.
2. **Should `claimantAlsoSource` ever refuse?** It is reported, because a contract may accept an agent's own trace. A relying party that forbids it has no expectation to say so yet.
3. **`records` over statement digests or payload digests.** Statement digests are proposed: they identify what the source signed.
4. **A source that serves many claims with the same inputs.** The listing is per inputs, so two claims with equal inputs share it. That seems right and should be said in the specification.
5. **Whether `wvp-0.2` should be deprecated** once `wvp-0.3` is Final. Not by this RFC.
